# Judge rubric: is a per-item count the CSV contradicts a FAIL?

Status: ready-for-human
Category: enhancement

## Observation

On C2 for 11-17, the reply said "Cedar Lane Books: 2 (orders 1062, 1077)", but the CSV has three cancelled orders for that customer (1062, 1077, 1111). That dropped a tied winner. The judge returned PASS, because the rubric says not to check arithmetic or fail a reply only because a number is wrong. Calibration example cal-01 sits on the same boundary.

## Proposal (not applied)

Clarify the rubric as follows. Totals and the final answer are out of scope, but a per-item fact the reply states about the data is graded as grounding. Examples are "customer X has 2 cancelled orders" or "order 1076 is not UPS".

## Comments

### Triage decision

**The rubric stays unchanged. The existing rubric already covers unsupported row-level claims, and per-group counts are excluded by design.** This stays `ready-for-human` because the only open question needs independent human labels.

**The three error types:**
- **Arithmetic error:** a count or sum computed wrongly from rows that are correctly identified. In the baseline these are P1 on all three files, P9 on 11-10, P5 on 11-17, and the "2" in C2.
- **Omission:** a qualifying row left out. In the baseline these are order 1076 in P4 on 11-10, and order 1111 in C2 on 11-17.
- **Unsupported row-level claim:** a stated fact about a specific row that the CSV contradicts or doesn't contain. Examples are an order with the wrong customer, status, carrier, date, or units, an order ID that doesn't exist, or an invented customer.

**What the rubric already covers:**
- **Grounding:** "every fact the reply states about the data (order IDs, customers, carriers, statuses, dates, and the filter or date range it says it used) matches the CSV". FAIL applies to "a fact the CSV contradicts or does not contain".
- That covers the third type. Calibration example cal-07 (an invented customer, with orders wrongly attributed to it) is labelled FAIL, and the judge agreed.
- The rubric explicitly excludes the first type: 'Counts, totals, and the final "Answer:" value are graded separately: do not check arithmetic, and do not fail or flag a reply only because a number is wrong.'
- It's silent on omissions, which aren't stated facts.

**Verification against the baseline reports:**
- I checked every order ID mentioned in the 45 replies against its CSV row, covering customer, status, carrier, dates, and units, plus every grouping under a customer or carrier.
- No contradicted row-level fact was found. The checker was validated on planted errors first.
- So no judge PASS in the baseline hides an unsupported row-level claim.
- C2's row-level facts (1062 and 1077 are Cedar Lane Books cancellations) are true. Its error is an omission plus the resulting count, and both are outside the rubric by design. `answer_correct` caught it ("missing: Cedar Lane Books").
- The judge PASS on C2 therefore follows the rubric, and the failure analysis wording was corrected to say so.

**Known evaluator limitations:**
- A per-group count such as "Cedar Lane Books: 2" sits on the boundary between arithmetic and a fact about the data. The rubric resolves it as arithmetic, so the judge won't flag a wrong per-group count even when it changes the answer to a tie question. Only `answer_correct` protects those cases.
- The judge can't detect omissions (see issue 03).
- The boundary has a single calibration example (cal-01), labelled by the coding agent rather than an independent human.
- The judge tolerates self-corrected explanations.
  - The C2 reply on 11-10 first states "Cedar Lane Books: 2 cancelled orders (orders 1037, 1062, 1077 -> wait, … = 3 cancelled orders)", then re-counts correctly. The judge passed it.
  - The rubric's FLAG covers "a claim could reasonably be read either way", but a reply that contradicts itself and then corrects itself isn't named explicitly.
  - The final counts and the answer were correct, so the case verdict is right. The explanation's presentation is a product-quality issue the judge doesn't grade.

**Justified future improvements (not applied):**
- Have an independent human relabel cal-01, and label a C2-style reply (correct rows, wrong per-group count) in a separate second calibration round.
- Change the rubric's count sentence only if those human labels disagree with it. Then re-run calibration once and record the change in the design document.

### Human brief

**Category:** enhancement

**Summary:** decide, from independent human labels, whether the judge rubric should grade per-group counts the CSV contradicts.

**Current behaviour:**
- The rubric grades row-level facts (grounding) and excludes counts, totals, and the final value.
- A wrong per-group count, such as "Cedar Lane Books: 2" when the CSV has 3, therefore passes the judge. Only `answer_correct` catches it, when it changes the answer.

**Desired behaviour:** the rubric's treatment of per-group counts matches an independent human's judgement, as shown by calibration agreement on examples at this boundary.

**Key interfaces:**
- the rubric's sentence on counts and totals
- the calibration label set (a new, separate second round, so the first run stays reproducible)
- the calibration runner, which is re-run once

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] An independent human relabels cal-01 and labels at least two new per-group-count examples, including one C2-style reply.
- [ ] If the human labels agree with the current rubric, the rubric is left unchanged and the agreement is recorded.
- [ ] If they disagree, the count sentence is revised once, calibration is re-run once, and the change and new agreement are recorded in the design document.

**Out of scope:**
- grading totals or the final Answer value in the judge
- any change to `answer_correct`
- tuning the rubric against the 45 baseline results

**Why this can't go to an agent:** the decision depends on human judgement labels and a rubric policy choice.
